E-commerce usability study

Observing where shoppers lose time and focus in dense online shopping interfaces.

Research question

How do cluttered layouts, long pages, and unclear navigation affect common shopping tasks?

Method

Our team conducted task-based usability testing with three people: one on mobile and two on desktop. Participants searched for products, compared options, read reviews, added items to a cart, and moved through checkout. We observed navigation paths, scrolling, task time, and frustration points.

Findings and response

Our observations highlighted excess scrolling on mobile, many competing options, and confusing paths. The team compared a minimal interface, a guided shopping assistant, and a comparison-focused interface, then selected the minimal direction to reduce clutter and improve navigation.

This was a small, qualitative class study. It identified usability themes; it did not establish a statistically generalizable improvement or measure a shipped redesign.
